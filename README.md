# Funded Slots Roadmap

One static page: how many prop-firm evals to buy per firm today, what they cost, and the cash plan for the next 12 weeks.

Live page: https://3bdotrades.github.io/ (G1), https://3bdotrades.github.io/?fleet=v2 (V2 fleet) and https://3bdotrades.github.io/?fleet=sim (Simulation).

A fleet's accounts and eval signals are read from the fleet dashboard each time the page opens: how many evals, funded and flip accounts each firm has, and where each one stands (an eval's profit so far, a funded account's aim, total and room, a flip account's winning days, room and cycle total). The plan never holds more evals than the fleet's highest number of eval signals in a day. Prices, payouts and each size's flip room are set in `index.html`.

The plan starts today. The fleet's trading day rolls at 18:00 ET and its accounts trade all through it, so the dashboard also says which accounts have traded today already (the date of each account's last trade). Such an account takes its next trade tomorrow; an eval bought today starts tomorrow for the share of today's eval trades that is already in. In the hour after midnight here, at a weekend, or with numbers kept from an earlier day, every account trades on the plan's first day. A tab left open past midnight loads itself again, so that its plan starts on the new day.

A firm lets 5 accounts trade at once, all its sizes together. The Slots box of a firm sets a smaller number, 0 to 5: the plan then fills only that many places there and buys the evals for them (accounts already trading above it go on until they are done). The Simulation tab has the same box.

A size the plan buys evals for today carries a warning under its row: buy them today, what they cost, the payouts expected of them (how often an eval ends in an account in profit, times what such an account books), and what their eval seats make a day while those evals run (the expected payouts less the cost, over the days an eval lasts), which is what a day with the seats empty does not make. These are expected values: most single evals end with nothing, and it is profit not made, not cash paid out. A size with nothing to buy, or an unticked one, has no warning.

What you change on the page is the same in every browser that opens it: the firms you untick and the slots you type in a fleet's view, and the simulation's rates, days, evals, firms and slots. There is no key and no sign-in, so anyone with the page can change them. An edit goes to a public relay (ntfy.sh), which keeps it for 12 hours, and every open page asks the relay when it opens, when its tab is shown again and every two minutes. `tools/share.js` runs with the counter below and writes what the relay holds into `shared.json` on the `track` branch, which a page reads when it opens, so an edit stays after the relay has forgotten it. A browser that already had boxes unticked, slots typed or a simulation set up shares them the first time it opens the page, if nobody has shared any yet. A tab still open on an older copy of the page does not know the slots, and an edit made there puts them back to 5 for everyone; reloading that tab ends it.

The four Live rates cannot be typed: they are the fleet's own counts. Evals passed of those finished, funded accounts that reached profit of those finished, and flip days won on 50K and on 25K accounts. When the dashboard carries its Lifetime table, the page reads the counts from it. When it does not, they come from `tools/track.js`: a job (`.github/workflows/track.yml`, every 10 minutes) that reads every build of the dashboard from its public repository and counts on from the last Lifetime table the dashboard showed (10 Nov 2026, 19:47 UTC). Its counts, and a log of what it counted and when, are in `g1.json` and `v2.json` on the `track` branch. The same counter also runs every 10 minutes as a scheduled task on the owner's PC, for the times GitHub's timer does not start the job; whichever saves a build first, the other finds nothing left to do.

An eval has no fixed length. It trades one day at a time, passes when its profit reaches its target and ends when it loses two days in a row. A winning day is worth half the target (the 50% consistency rule), so it takes at least 2 days; at Tradeify the rule is 40%, so it takes at least 3 and passes less often. The win rate per day comes from the fleet's live eval pass rate; Tradeify's smaller winning day has a closer target, so its days win more often, by as much as chance says.

A flip account's first two payouts are $1,800 and then half of it on a 50K, $900 and then $450 on a 25K; Tradeify (Select Flex) pays $2,250 and $1,125 first. 1% of every payout goes in fees; at Topstep it is a fixed $30 a payout instead. A payout is accepted once its winning days are in and the account has any room at all: it takes its usual amount, or all the room there is when that is less, and an account left at zero is done.

After its second payout the account goes on. With $1,000 of room or more left ($500 on a 25K) it flips 5 more winning days for a third payout, which takes half of its room: about $1,000 of room is a $500 request and pays $450. After that third payout, or straight after the second when less is left, it is used again for one funded trade, half the size of a first one: it risks $500 for $2,000 on a 50K and for $1,500 on a 25K (the sizes on V2's dashboard). If that trade wins, the account starts over: 5 winning days to a payout of its first size, then the second, and so on. If it loses, the account is done. On Chance the trade wins 20% of the time (25% on a 25K); with a fleet's rates it wins as often as that fleet's funded trades would at those odds (G1's funded trade has the same odds; V2's has longer ones, so its used-again trade wins a little more often than its funded trades). A run follows an account through three such wins. All that time the account keeps its place among the 5 a firm lets trade at once.

At Tradeify a payout after the first also needs the flips since the last payout to be at least $1 up. An account with its winning days in and less than that gets no payout yet: it takes funded trades for what is missing until one wins and the payout is due, or its room is gone.

The dashboard shows "n/5 days" for every cycle after the first payout, so such an account is taken to be in its second cycle. A funded account is read from its row: a new one has two tries, one that has lost a trade has one left, and one with less room than a new funded account starts with is a flip account that was sent back.

G1 also holds funded accounts that are not on the dashboard yet (on 10 Nov 2026: two Lucid 50K, two Lucid 25K and one Topstep, set in `index.html`). The counts files note every new funded account and every pass the dashboard shows, and the page takes those off the held ones as they come onto the dashboard.

The Simulation tab starts from zero: tick the firms, type the evals for each one or a total, pick the days, and it shows cost, payouts and net. It opens on Chance: the rates a trader with no edge gets from the trade sizes alone, stop / (stop + target) on the price moves. An eval day risks $1,000 for $1,520 (39.7% a day; $1,210 at Tradeify, 45.2%), a funded trade $1,000 for $4,000 (20%), a flip day 15 points for 16 on a 50K (48.4%) and for 10.75 on a 25K (58.3%). G1's or V2's live rates, with that fleet's eval signals, can be picked instead.

The cash chart of a fleet has a dashed line, Coin flip: the same 12 weeks from today (the accounts the fleet holds, where each one stands, its eval seats, the firms unticked and the slots typed) with every trade at chance instead of the fleet's live rates: the Chance rates above, with the fleet's own funded trade (G1's is $1,000 for $4,000, 1 in 5; V2's $1,000 for $5,000, 1 in 6). Where the running total is above the dashed line the fleet's trades do better than chance, where it is below they do worse. The weeks of the history have no such line.
