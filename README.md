# Funded Slots Roadmap

One static page: how many prop-firm evals to buy per firm today, what they cost, and the cash plan for the next 12 weeks.

Live page: https://3bdotrades.github.io/ (G1), https://3bdotrades.github.io/?fleet=v2 (V2 fleet) and https://3bdotrades.github.io/?fleet=5ers (5ers). (The Simulation tab was taken off on 17 Nov 2026: an old ?fleet=sim link opens G1.)

A fleet's accounts and eval signals are read from the fleet dashboard each time the page opens, and again every 5 minutes while its tab is shown (and when it is shown again after that): how many evals, funded and flip accounts each firm has, and where each one stands (an eval's profit so far, a funded account's aim, total and room, a flip account's winning days, room and cycle total). The plan never holds more evals than the fleet's highest number of eval signals in a day. Prices, payouts and each size's flip room are set in `index.html`. A firm or size the dashboard lists that has none set there (V2's "LUCID_DAILY", shown as Lucid Daily 50K, since 20 Nov 2026, and a Lucid 100K eval) has its accounts counted and shown, and the plan buys no evals there: its row says "no rules yet" until its rules are set.

The plan starts today. The fleet's trading day rolls at 18:00 ET and its accounts trade all through it, so the dashboard also says which accounts have traded today already (the date of each account's last trade, or a day P&L that is not zero). Until 16 Nov 2026 its footer said the trading day; since then it says only when the page was built ("Updated 08:17 ET"), and the day is taken from that time: the last such time at or before now in New York, the next day from 18:00 ET on, and a Saturday or a Sunday is the Monday after (the rule that gives the day every one of the 1,763 builds that said it gave). Such an account takes its next trade tomorrow; an eval bought today starts tomorrow for the share of today's eval trades that is already in. In the hour after midnight here, at a weekend, or with numbers kept from an earlier day, every account trades on the plan's first day. A tab left open past midnight loads itself again, so that its plan starts on the new day.

Since 20 Nov 2026 G1 and V2 are locked like the 5ers tab: no tick box and no Sync button; the firms unticked are shown as they are kept (as "skipped") and change only through a session. A switch in `index.html` (FLEET_LOCK) brings the tick boxes back. The evals to buy are shown for the week (NEXT_WEEK): the "Next week" column and the "Buy next week" total add up the 5 trading days from the next buy day (Monday to Friday at a weekend; today and the next 4 trading days on a weekday), and the warning under a firm speaks of next week. Each account size with planning rules has its own editable Slots limit from 0 to 5 (SLOTS_OPEN). A firm can have up to five accounts trading across all sizes. An older shared limit applies until you change a row for that firm. Each size starts with the old value and keeps it until you edit that row. The plan uses the slot limits when buying evals and starting funded trades. Accounts already trading above the limit continue until they finish.

A size the plan buys evals for today carries a warning under its row: buy them today, what they cost, the payouts expected of them (how often an eval ends in an account in profit, times what such an account books), and what their eval seats make a day while those evals run (the expected payouts less the cost, over the days an eval lasts), which is what a day with the seats empty does not make. These are expected values: most single evals end with nothing, and it is profit not made, not cash paid out. A size with nothing to buy, or an unticked one, has no warning.

What you change on the page is the same in every browser that opens it: the firms you untick and the slots you type in a fleet's view, and everything typed on the 5ers tab. There is no key and no sign-in, so anyone with the page can change them. An edit goes to a public relay (ntfy.sh), which keeps it for 12 hours, and every open page asks the relay when it opens, when its tab is shown again and every two minutes. `tools/share.js` runs with the counter below and writes what the relay holds into `shared.json` on the `track` branch, which a page reads when it opens, so an edit stays after the relay has forgotten it. A browser that already had boxes unticked, slots typed or a simulation set up shares them the first time it opens the page, if nobody has shared any yet. Reload older tabs to edit slots per size.

The four Live rates cannot be typed: they are the fleet's own counts. Evals passed of those finished, funded accounts that reached profit of those finished, and flip days won on 50K and on 25K accounts. When the dashboard carries its Lifetime table, the page reads the counts from it. When it does not, they come from `tools/track.js`: a job (`.github/workflows/track.yml`, every 10 minutes) that reads every build of the dashboard from its public repository and counts on from the last Lifetime table the dashboard showed (10 Nov 2026, 19:47 UTC). Its counts, and a log of what it counted and when, are in `g1.json` and `v2.json` on the `track` branch. The same counter also runs every 10 minutes as a scheduled task on the owner's PC, for the times GitHub's timer does not start the job; whichever saves a build first, the other finds nothing left to do. A build whose fleet section it cannot read is passed over, and the file notes since when (`unread`) until it reads one again; when that has lasted more than half an hour, the page says "Live rates not counted since …: the dashboard changed." (From 16 Nov 2026 21:56 UTC every build was passed over, as the footer no longer said the trading day, and the rates stood still with no word of it until 20 Nov, when the counter took the day from the build's time, by the rule above, and both fleets were counted again from the last build it had read.) Until a browser has read the counts, the four rates and the plan show "–" (before 20 Nov 2026 a new browser showed the counts of 10 Nov as live meanwhile), and the kept counts are taken even when the dashboard itself cannot be read.

An eval has no fixed length. It trades one day at a time, passes when its profit reaches its target and ends when it loses two days in a row. A winning day is worth half the target (the 50% consistency rule), so it takes at least 2 days; at Tradeify the rule is 40%, so it takes at least 3 and passes less often. The win rate per day comes from the fleet's live eval pass rate; Tradeify's smaller winning day has a closer target, so its days win more often, by as much as chance says.

A flip account's first two payouts are $1,800 and then half of it on a 50K, $900 and then $450 on a 25K; Tradeify (Select Flex) pays $2,250 and $1,125 first. 1% of every payout goes in fees; at Topstep it is a fixed $30 a payout instead. A payout is accepted once its winning days are in and the account has any room at all: it takes its usual amount, or all the room there is when that is less, and an account left at zero is done.

After its second payout the account goes on. With $1,000 of room or more left ($500 on a 25K) it flips 5 more winning days for a third payout, which takes half of its room: about $1,000 of room is a $500 request and pays $450. After that third payout, or straight after the second when less is left, it is used again for one funded trade, half the size of a first one: it risks $500 for $2,000 on a 50K and for $1,500 on a 25K (the sizes on V2's dashboard). If that trade wins, the account starts over: 5 winning days to a payout of its first size, then the second, and so on. If it loses, the account is done. On Chance the trade wins 20% of the time (25% on a 25K); with a fleet's rates it wins as often as that fleet's funded trades would at those odds (G1's funded trade has the same odds; V2's has longer ones, so its used-again trade wins a little more often than its funded trades). A run follows an account through three such wins. All that time the account keeps its place among the 5 a firm lets trade at once.

At Tradeify a payout after the first also needs the flips since the last payout to be at least $1 up. An account with its winning days in and less than that gets no payout yet: it takes funded trades for what is missing until one wins and the payout is due, or its room is gone.

The dashboard shows "n/5 days" for every cycle after the first payout, so such an account is taken to be in its second cycle. A funded account is read from its row: a new one has two tries, one that has lost a trade has one left, and one with less room than a new funded account starts with is a flip account that was sent back.

G1 also holds funded accounts that are not on the dashboard yet (on 10 Nov 2026: two Lucid 50K, two Lucid 25K and one Topstep, set in `index.html`). The counts files note every new funded account and every pass the dashboard shows, and the page takes those off the held ones as they come onto the dashboard.

The 5ers tab shows two profiles at the same time, side by side (one under the other on a narrow screen): Profile 1, the owner's, which his bot feeds, and Profile 2, typed on the page. Each has its own journal, Paid, Received, My record, payouts on hold, hub credits, accounts and plan, kept and shared apart (`?fleet=5ers&p=1` and `&p=2` open one alone); Profile 2 started empty on 19 Nov 2026. The 5ers tab plans 48 weeks (its Net tile and its per-size column; G1 and V2 keep 12). Its cash chart ends 4 weeks after the running total turns into a steady line: after the first week from which the average weekly net (payouts in less evals out) of the 4 weeks up to each week stays within 10% of the steady week (Once running, a week) to the end of the 48; at least 8 weeks, and all 48 when it never settles. Its Cash needed shows two numbers: first the wallet to keep ready, the deepest cash hole that 99 runs in 100 stay within when the plan is played 5,000 times with every trade won or lost at random at its rates, from the profile as it is, 26 weeks from today; under it the plan's own deepest point, its expected running total at its lowest. Since 20 Nov 2026 nothing on it can be typed or clicked, on both profiles: the accounts' stages, the payouts due with their amounts and days, the slots and the hub credits are shown as text. It changes from the owner's bot (tools/five.js) and the payout days, or through a session; G1 and V2 keep their tick boxes and slots boxes. Since 20 Nov 2026 the Journal card is not shown (what it holds is still counted, and the bot's results and "Bought" still go into it), the hub credits are shown and not typed, and My record shows, for the eval pass and the funded win, first the backtest (its days on the heading) and under it the profile's own rate from its journal ("none yet" with no result; the counts only on the hover). Signals a day, shown once above the two profiles, is the system's signals a trading day when it takes one trade at a time: every G1 and V2 signal from 13 Jul 2026, each walked to the bot's funded target (a 25-point stop and a 57-point target), counted when it fills 60 seconds or more after the trade counted before it has ended. It grows by a day each trading day: a script on the owner's PC counts every day that has ended, and the PC task publishes its summary as `signals.json` on the `track` branch (`tools/signals.js`: dates and counts only, and nothing when the script's own check fails). The page shows that file, else the last one the browser saw, else the summary of 19 Nov 2026 (91 days: 19.59 a day, median 19, fewest 5, most 43).

The 5ers tab is one firm that is not on the dashboard and has rules of its own, so everything there comes from what you enter: a journal of what was done, the slots of each size and the accounts you hold. It has two sizes, at most 5 accounts of each. An eval is one trade that risks $2,000 to make $3,000 ($1,000 to make $1,500 on a 25K): a win passes it, and it is funded and takes its funded trade on the next trading day; a loss ends it. A funded trade risks the same to make $4,500 ($2,250 on a 25K; the bot's funded rule since 17 Nov 2026, a 25-point stop and a 56.25-point target): a loss ends the account, a win pays three payouts of $1,158 ($579 on a 25K), 14, 28 and 42 days after the day the account was funded. On the trading day after the third payout the account takes one more funded trade, whose win is paid 14, 28 and 42 days after that last payout. An account that ended is replaced by a new eval on the next trading day. The bot's targets since 18 Nov 2026 are a 25-point stop and a 38.25-point target for an eval's trade, 57 for a funded one: with no edge an eval's trade wins 25 / 63.25 = 39.53% of the time and a funded one 25 / 82 = 30.49% (chance, the chart's dashed line).

What an eval costs: its list price ($100 a 50K, $59 a 25K) less the hub credits used, then 20% off. Every eval bought and every eval passed earns hub credits ($10 a 50K, $5 a 25K), and they go into the next evals. The Hub credits box holds the credits you have (50 on 18 Nov 2026): an eval bought costs 0.8 x (its list price less the credits held, as far as they go), so a 50K with 50 credits costs $40 and with none $80; the balance goes down by what was used and up by what a buy and a pass earn, and the evals the plan buys today cost that much less. For the evals after those the plan takes off the credits of a buy and the credits of a pass as often as an eval passes (at the backtest's 38.69%: $68.90 and $41.65). Credits are not real money: they lower what evals cost and are not counted as paid or received; a payout taken as hub credits is counted as received.

The Journal card holds a line for everything done, each with its day: evals bought (how many of a size and what was paid), evals passed and evals lost (how many of a size), funded trades won and lost (how many), and payouts in cash or in hub credits. Paid, Received and My record are counted from it and cannot be typed: Paid is what was paid for evals, Received the payouts, the eval pass rate the evals passed of those passed or lost, the funded win rate the funded trades won of those won or lost (at the backtest while there are fewer than 20 of a kind). The plan runs at the system's backtest until the profile's own record has 20 results of a kind, then at that record. The backtest is every G1 and V2 GO of the days Signals a day covers, walked at the bot's targets, rate = won / (won + lost), flats left out (13 Jul – 19 Nov 2026, 91 days: eval pass 38.69%, 1,105 won and 1,751 lost; funded win 29.51%, 841 won and 2,009 lost). It comes with Signals a day in `signals.json` and follows its days. The newest line is on top, what was before the journal at the bottom. The first row adds a line, the × of a line takes it out after asking once more. "Bought" on an empty account row does two things at once: the row becomes an Eval and its price becomes a line of today. Picking Eval in a row's list is for an account already held and adds no money. The tab started again on 17 Nov 2026 evening with the owner's state: five 50K evals; on the 25K one payout of $965 due 26 Nov, three of $578 due 1, 15 and 29 Dec, and three evals; the 8 evals as bought that day for $400 and $141.60, so Paid $541.60 and Received $0.00, and My record from zero. What was kept before that is not read again. It keeps its newest 100 dated lines; older ones fold into the "before the journal" lines of their kind, so every total and count stays. The plan's evals to buy, cash needed and weeks ahead take the journal's rates; the chart starts from its So far.

The results of the owner's bot come in by themselves (`tools/five.js`, run on his PC with the counter every 10 minutes; the tab asks for news every half minute): a new eval account is an eval bought (an empty place becomes an Eval, its price by the rule above goes into the journal), a finished trade is an eval passed or lost or a funded trade won or lost (the place moves to Funded, to 3 payouts due with the first 14 days after the trade, or to empty). Only the kind of result, its day and the account's size leave the PC. An eval you record yourself before the bot has seen its account ("Bought", or a Bought line) waits for it: when the bot sees a new eval of that size within three days, that is the same eval and it is not counted again. An account BlackArrow's log shows blocked outside a trade of the bot's (blown) counts the same as a lost trade, once, when it was bought since 18 Nov 2026. A payout goes into Received the day after its day, and its account steps down: 3 payouts due, 2, 1, then Funded, which trades again (on both profiles). Anything paid that is not an eval the bot saw still goes in through a session. Payouts on hold lists every payout still due of the accounts, in the order of their days, and what they come to.

Each 5ers account is entered with its stage: an eval, a funded account, or a funded win with three, two or one payouts still due, with the day of the next one (a day that has passed counts as today) and what each payout still due brings (the size's usual payout unless another amount is typed). An eval or a funded account takes its trade on the plan's first trading day; after 14:30 in New York, the plan's last entry, that is the next trading day, and an eval is still bought today. A size with fewer slots than accounts keeps the first of them in their places; the others run until they end and are not replaced. The chart's dashed line is the same accounts with the rates at chance (39.53% and 30.49%), drawn when the plan's rates are not chance. The warning under a size gives what its evals cost, the payouts expected of the accounts they become, and what their places make a trading day in the long run: a new eval's expected payouts less its price, over the trading days until the place needs its next eval.

The cash chart of a fleet has a dashed line, Coin flip: the same 12 weeks from today (the accounts the fleet holds, where each one stands, its eval seats, the firms unticked and the slots typed) with every trade at chance instead of the fleet's live rates: the rates a trader with no edge gets from the trade sizes alone, stop / (stop + target) on the price moves. An eval day risks $1,000 for $1,520 (39.7% a day; $1,210 at Tradeify, 45.2%), a flip day 15 points for 16 on a 50K (48.4%) and for 10.75 on a 25K (58.3%), and the funded trade is the fleet's own (G1's is $1,000 for $4,000, 1 in 5; V2's $1,000 for $5,000, 1 in 6). Where the running total is above the dashed line the fleet's trades do better than chance, where it is below they do worse. The weeks of the history have no such line.

Since 23 Nov 2026 the 5ers tab plans new evals under the5ers' new program (their rules of 22 Nov 2026): list prices $97 / $57, an eval needs 6% and 5 profitable days, a funded account is paid at 4% with 5 new profitable days and no day above 40% of its profit, at most 5 payouts with the caps $1,250 / $1,500 / $1,500 / $1,750 / $1,750 on a 50K ($750 / $875 / $1,000 / $1,000 / $1,000 on a 25K) before the 80% split and the 3.5% fee. Such accounts are kept as `E` (eval) and `F` (funded) in the accounts, each played a day at a time by the bot's day plan (`FIVE_NEW`, `fiveChain` in `index.html`; expected cash = the average of 2,000 seeded runs). Funded accounts and payouts from before keep the plan of before; when one of them ends, the eval that takes its place is a new-program one. Every payout needs a new 4% of profit since the last one (the owner, 23 Nov 2026, confirmed with the5ers).
